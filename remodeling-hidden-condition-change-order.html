Remodeling field guide

A clean hidden-condition change order for remodeling work

Walls, floors, and cabinets can conceal conditions that were not reasonably visible during estimating. The record should not turn that uncertainty into a vague blank check. It should document the observation, the investigation boundary, the proposed next work, and the exact decision needed now.

Use this as an operational example, not legal language. Contract, signature, tax, notice, cancellation, licensing, and record-retention requirements vary by project and jurisdiction.

Pause and preserve the observation

  • Photograph the exposed condition before covering or removing more than safety requires.
  • Record where it was found and which planned task exposed it.
  • Separate observed facts from a suspected cause.
  • Identify immediate safety or weather-protection steps that cannot wait.

Define the decision-sized scope

Break a large unknown into an approvable step. For example: “Remove the visibly deteriorated subfloor within the marked four-by-six-foot area, inspect the accessible joist faces, install equivalent subfloor material where framing is sound, and stop for a revised proposal if structural damage extends beyond that area.”

This is clearer than “repair hidden damage as needed,” because the client can see the boundary and the contractor has a stopping point.

Show what is not included

Name material exclusions, specialty trades, engineering, permits, testing, hazardous-material handling, code upgrades, finish matching, or work outside the exposed area when those items are not part of this decision. Do not use boilerplate to conceal costs that are already known.

Connect money to schedule

  • Show labor, material, equipment, subcontractor, tax, and credit lines that apply.
  • State whether this amount replaces an allowance or is added to the contract.
  • Give the estimated delay and name inspections, specialists, deliveries, or client selections that can extend it.
  • Update downstream milestone dates rather than stating only “plus three days.”

Version every revision

If opening the limited area reveals a larger condition, close the first issued decision as approved, declined, or revised and create a second change. Preserving both versions gives the project a chronological record instead of one document that silently changes over time.
