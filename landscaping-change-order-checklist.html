Landscaping contractor checklist

Landscaping changes without the text-message confusion

Landscape work changes quickly when quantities, access, drainage, buried conditions, plant availability, or client preferences differ from the estimate. A short written approval can keep one request from becoming a disagreement about what the original price covered.

Use this as an operational example, not legal language. Contract, signature, tax, notice, cancellation, licensing, and record-retention requirements vary by project and jurisdiction.

Identify the trigger

  • Client addition: another bed, tree, irrigation zone, edging run, lighting fixture, or haul-away task.
  • Quantity difference: actual soil, stone, mulch, sod, or disposal volume differs from the estimate’s stated basis.
  • Site condition: buried debris, unsuitable soil, access limits, drainage, roots, utilities, or grade conditions change the work.
  • Substitution: a specified plant or material is unavailable and the client must accept an alternative.

Use measurable scope

Replace phrases such as “add more mulch” with quantity, area, depth, material, color, and placement. For planting, record species or approved substitute, container size, count, location, and whether establishment care or replacement warranty changes.

Separate price components

  • Additional labor and equipment time
  • Material quantity and unit assumption
  • Delivery, disposal, mobilization, or subcontractor charges
  • Credits for removed work or unavailable selections
  • Applicable tax and the exact net change

State dependencies honestly

Weather, utility marking, inspections, access, supplier inventory, and client selections can affect dates. Record the best current schedule impact and the dependency instead of promising an unconditional completion date.

Close the loop in the field

Send one review containing the scope, price, schedule impact, and exclusions. Do not treat silence as approval. Capture approve, decline, or revision requested, keep the returned receipt, and give the client the same final version.
