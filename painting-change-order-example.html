Painting change order: hidden wall damage after prep begins

A useful painting change order separates newly discovered preparation from the original painting scope. It tells the client what was found, why it was not included, what additional work is proposed, and what happens if they decline. The example below is deliberately factual instead of adversarial.

Use this as an operational example, not legal language. Contract, signature, tax, notice, cancellation, licensing, and record-retention requirements vary by project and jurisdiction.

Example situation

The estimate covers cleaning, spot filling, one primer coat, and two finish coats. After a built-in cabinet is removed, the crew finds delaminated drywall paper and a soft water-damaged section that cannot be coated reliably as-is.

Original-scope reference

Original scope: Prepare and paint the accessible kitchen walls and ceiling. Minor nail holes and ordinary surface imperfections are included. Drywall replacement and concealed water damage are excluded.

Requested change

Added work: Cut out the damaged drywall section, confirm the cavity is dry, install a matching patch, tape, apply compound in the necessary coats, sand, spot-prime, and blend the finish coats into the affected wall. Plumbing, mold remediation, structural work, and repair of an active leak remain excluded.

That exclusions sentence matters: it keeps one visible wall repair from being mistaken for an open-ended promise to solve every cause behind it.

Price and schedule fields

  • List added labor separately from drywall, compound, primer, and other materials.
  • Show taxes or credits according to the contractor’s reviewed calculation.
  • State the expected added workdays and drying time.
  • Identify dependencies, such as confirming an active leak is repaired before closing the wall.

Decision record

Issue the change before patching begins when practical. Let the client approve, decline, or request a revision. If the client changes the size of the repair or chooses a different finish after reviewing it, issue a new version rather than editing the already-approved record.
