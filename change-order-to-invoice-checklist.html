Match the approved extra to the invoice—not to your memory.

An approved change order explains the extra work. An invoice requests payment. Before billing, connect each proposed invoice line to the exact approved scope and check what has already been billed, credited, or paid.

Operational guidance, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Approval alone does not establish that payment is due today. Your agreement, completion milestones, applicable rules, and any unresolved conditions determine when and how you can bill. This checklist does not create a right to collect payment.

1. Assemble the evidence before drafting the invoice

  • The original agreement and its payment schedule.
  • The exact issued change proposal, including reference, revision, scope, exclusions, price, and timing.
  • The decision receipt and any conditions or related correspondence.
  • Records of work performed and the applicable billing milestone.
  • Earlier invoices, credit notes, and payment records for the same extra.

A proposal, an approval, a completed task, an invoice, and a payment are different records. Mark each status separately. Never interpret a sent review link or a lack of response as approval.

2. Match one current approved version

Check the reference and revision on the receipt against the proposal you intend to bill. If a replacement was issued, retain both versions and resolve which one governs. Do not add the old proposal total to the replacement total unless the agreement clearly makes them separate additions.

A decision note may introduce a condition. Resolve any conflicting scope, price, or timing before treating it as an unconditional approval. A cryptographic signature can detect changes to a receipt; it does not establish the responder's real-world identity or authority.

The client says yes, but you cannot find the receipt

Do not turn a missing record into an invented approval. In ExtraOK, sharing a review link and receiving a decision in your own browser are separate steps. Before using an approval as the basis for an invoice, check whether the returned receipt was actually imported for the correct proposal.

  1. Identify the proposal first. Confirm the project, change reference, revision, scope, and amount with the customer. A yes to an earlier version does not settle a later change.
  2. Look for the existing decision. Check the private correspondence and retained receipt files for that version. If the customer still has the original receipt, ask them to return it through your agreed private channel. Do not ask for passwords, card details, or access to their browser.
  3. Use the receipt-import workflow. Import the returned receipt and check the decision shown on the dashboard. Read any conditions alongside it. If verification fails, keep the original file and resolve the mismatch; do not edit its contents or recreate a supposedly signed receipt.
  4. Record uncertainty honestly. If the receipt cannot be recovered, mark the approval evidence as missing. Clarify the current scope and obtain an explicit, accurately dated decision through an appropriate process. Never backdate it or describe it as proof of an earlier decision. Seek qualified advice where work already performed or a dispute makes the next step uncertain.

Message you can adapt: “I have your message about change [reference/revision], but I have not verified the decision receipt in my records. If you retained the receipt for that version, please return it through [private channel]. Otherwise, let us confirm the exact scope and any conditions before deciding the next step.”

A recovered or newly recorded approval still does not prove completion, payment, or that an invoice is due. Return to the agreed billing milestone and reconciliation checks below. ExtraOK does not recover deleted browser data from a cloud account.

4. Check common exceptions

  • Deposit already collected: verify its allocation under the agreement. Do not confuse payment received with the amount previously invoiced.
  • Partial completion: confirm whether the payment schedule permits a progress invoice. Do not bill the entire approved extra merely because approval exists.
  • Scope removed or reduced: retain the revised agreement and document any applicable credit. Do not silently rewrite the original record.
  • Declined or undecided proposal: resolve authorization rather than relabeling it as approved on an invoice.
  • Tax uncertainty: check the applicable treatment with a qualified adviser. The proposal's label is not a tax determination.

Part of the approved extra is finished. What can you invoice?

Do not convert a rough completion percentage into an amount due. An approved total does not necessarily price each task separately, and buying materials does not by itself establish a billing milestone. Use the agreed payment basis and your work records—not a new split invented on the invoice.

  1. Separate finished work from remaining work. Record the actual completed scope, the evidence reference, and what is still outstanding. Distinguish materials ordered, materials delivered, work installed, and any required acceptance; these are not interchangeable statuses.
  2. Find the agreed billing trigger. Check whether the contract permits a progress payment, a stated milestone, or billing only on completion. If the approved extra has one bundled price and no agreed allocation, clarify the basis before assigning a price to its components.
  3. Reconcile the proposed line. Compare it with earlier invoices, deposits, credits, and payment allocations. Keep the remaining scope visible so that a later invoice does not charge again for work already billed.
  4. Resolve an unclear split explicitly. Record the proposed allocation and obtain any agreement required through the appropriate process. Do not edit the old approval receipt or treat silence as agreement. Where the contract or dispute is unclear, obtain qualified advice before billing.

If the customer flags an invoice mismatch

Start with the disputed line, not a demand for another approval. A customer may be pointing to a duplicate charge, a different revision, an unmet condition, or a payment allocation that your records missed. This is a manual reconciliation process—not an ExtraOK billing or dispute-resolution feature.

  1. Identify the exact discrepancy. Record the invoice and line reference, the customer's description of the mismatch, and the proposal revision they are comparing it with. Acknowledge the question without treating it as agreement with the rest of the invoice.
  2. Compare the retained records. Match the issued proposal, decision receipt, billing milestone, previous invoices, credits, and payment allocation. Preserve the original files; do not edit an old approval to make it fit the invoice.
  3. Separate corrections from new work. Correct an invoice error through your bookkeeping process, retaining a reference to the original. If the requested work itself changes, prepare a distinct revised proposal and obtain an explicit decision rather than using the invoice as a substitute for approval.
  4. Send a specific reconciliation. State which line was checked, which records support the explanation, what correction was made if any, and what remains unresolved. Share only the relevant records through a private channel.
  5. Record the outcome without overstating it. Keep the response and corrected-document references. An explanation sent is not a dispute resolved; an approval receipt is not a payment receipt. If agreement remains unclear, seek qualified advice on the appropriate next step rather than inventing fees, deadlines, or collection rights.

A neutral clarification message

Thank you for flagging invoice [reference], line [reference]. I understand the mismatch concerns [specific issue]. I will compare it with change [reference/revision], the billing milestone, and prior invoice, credit, and payment records. Please identify any different version or record you are using. I will follow up by [realistic date] with the explanation, any correction, and any question still open.

Use only details you have verified and a follow-up date you can meet. Do not ask the customer to resend card or banking details. Keep payment information in the appropriate payment or accounting system.

Close the paperwork loop without losing the evidence

After reconciling the invoice, leave a short handoff record that another person can follow. This is a manual record for your existing business files, not an automated ExtraOK closeout feature. Keep approval, completion, billing, and payment as separate statuses—even when they concern the same change.

  1. Freeze the references, not the unresolved questions. List the issued proposal and revision, decision receipt, invoice, and any correction or credit. Preserve originals alongside replacements. Mark which version is current without deleting the earlier version.
  2. State what is actually verified. Write the scope-completion status from the work record and the payment status from your accounting or payment system. An invoice sent is not a payment received. If allocation or settlement is uncertain, say so.
  3. Assign the remaining follow-up. Give each open question an owner and a realistic review date. Do not label the change closed simply because the customer has not replied.
  4. Keep a usable recovery copy. Export the ExtraOK backup and retain the supporting documents separately in an access-controlled location. Confirm that the files can be opened. A review link alone is not an archive; do not test a restore over your only working copy.

Share the evidence, not your entire project archive

An invoice question rarely requires every customer record. Before forwarding an approval or handoff packet, decide what the recipient needs to check. This is a manual privacy checklist, not an ExtraOK access-control or secure-delivery service.

  1. Verify the recipient and purpose. Confirm the intended person and address through your established contact route. Do not send records to a new address merely because a message asks for them. Identify the exact change and revision needed for the question.
  2. Select the smallest useful packet. Include the relevant proposal, decision evidence, and invoice references only when needed. Do not send a full ExtraOK backup as a routine customer attachment: it may contain unrelated project records. Keep banking credentials, card details, passwords, and unrelated personal information out.
  3. Preserve signed originals. Do not edit a signed decision receipt to remove fields and then present the altered file as verifiable evidence. If an explanatory excerpt is appropriate, create a separate, clearly labeled redacted copy and retain the original privately. The excerpt is not a replacement for the original receipt or its verification.
  4. Use an agreed private channel and record the handoff. Check the attachments before sending; keep a note of the recipient, purpose, document versions, and send date. If your chosen sharing service supports expiry or restricted access, configure and verify those settings there. ExtraOK does not revoke files or copies already received.

If you send the wrong file: stop further sharing, use any available access-revocation controls in the delivery service, contact the recipient through a verified route, and assess what was exposed. Do not assume deletion is guaranteed. Obtain appropriate professional advice if contractual or legal notification duties may apply.

Return to the closeout record to retain the document references, or use the reconciliation worksheet to identify what still needs checking. A packet sent is not proof of receipt, agreement, completion, or payment.
