Match the approved extra to the invoice—not to your memory.

An approved change order explains the extra work. An invoice requests payment. Before billing, connect each proposed invoice line to the exact approved scope and check what has already been billed, credited, or paid.

Operational guidance, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Approval alone does not establish that payment is due today. Your agreement, completion milestones, applicable rules, and any unresolved conditions determine when and how you can bill. This checklist does not create a right to collect payment.

1. Assemble the evidence before drafting the invoice

  • The original agreement and its payment schedule.
  • The exact issued change proposal, including reference, revision, scope, exclusions, price, and timing.
  • The decision receipt and any conditions or related correspondence.
  • Records of work performed and the applicable billing milestone.
  • Earlier invoices, credit notes, and payment records for the same extra.

A proposal, an approval, a completed task, an invoice, and a payment are different records. Mark each status separately. Never interpret a sent review link or a lack of response as approval.

2. Match one current approved version

Check the reference and revision on the receipt against the proposal you intend to bill. If a replacement was issued, retain both versions and resolve which one governs. Do not add the old proposal total to the replacement total unless the agreement clearly makes them separate additions.

A decision note may introduce a condition. Resolve any conflicting scope, price, or timing before treating it as an unconditional approval. A cryptographic signature can detect changes to a receipt; it does not establish the responder's real-world identity or authority.

3. Use this reconciliation record for each extra

Copy these fields into your existing bookkeeping record. Keep sensitive customer and payment details in your appropriate business systems, not in a publicly shared review link.

Project and change reference
Which project, change number, and revision does this line concern?
Approved scope and amount
What was agreed, including exclusions, credits, and the stated tax treatment?
Decision evidence
Where are the exact reviewed proposal, decision, date, and any conditions retained?
Billing basis
Which agreed milestone permits this invoice, and what evidence supports it?
Previously billed
Which earlier invoice lines already cover this scope?
Credits and payments
Which recorded credits or payments apply, and how are they allocated?
Proposed invoice line
Does its description, reference, amount, and treatment match the agreed basis without duplication?
Unresolved exception
What must be clarified, by whom, before sending?

4. Check common exceptions

  • Deposit already collected: verify its allocation under the agreement. Do not confuse payment received with the amount previously invoiced.
  • Partial completion: confirm whether the payment schedule permits a progress invoice. Do not bill the entire approved extra merely because approval exists.
  • Scope removed or reduced: retain the revised agreement and document any applicable credit. Do not silently rewrite the original record.
  • Declined or undecided proposal: resolve authorization rather than relabeling it as approved on an invoice.
  • Tax uncertainty: check the applicable treatment with a qualified adviser. The proposal's label is not a tax determination.

Illustrative handoff: repair now, repaint later

This is a fictional workflow example, not a customer story. A client approved a wall repair and repainting proposal. The repair is complete, but painting is waiting on a separate scheduling decision. Before invoicing, the contractor checks whether the agreement permits billing the completed portion, checks prior invoices, and records the remaining work. Neither the approval receipt nor the completed repair alone answers when the entire amount becomes due.

A short note for the invoice handoff

Invoice [reference] includes work under approved change [reference/revision]. The line describes [scope] and corresponds to [agreed billing milestone]. Related earlier invoices or credits are [references, or none after checking]. Please let us know if you see a mismatch with the proposal or decision record.

Replace every bracket with verified details. Send the supporting record through an appropriate private channel; do not publish customer information.

If the customer flags an invoice mismatch

Start with the disputed line, not a demand for another approval. A customer may be pointing to a duplicate charge, a different revision, an unmet condition, or a payment allocation that your records missed. This is a manual reconciliation process—not an ExtraOK billing or dispute-resolution feature.

  1. Identify the exact discrepancy. Record the invoice and line reference, the customer's description of the mismatch, and the proposal revision they are comparing it with. Acknowledge the question without treating it as agreement with the rest of the invoice.
  2. Compare the retained records. Match the issued proposal, decision receipt, billing milestone, previous invoices, credits, and payment allocation. Preserve the original files; do not edit an old approval to make it fit the invoice.
  3. Separate corrections from new work. Correct an invoice error through your bookkeeping process, retaining a reference to the original. If the requested work itself changes, prepare a distinct revised proposal and obtain an explicit decision rather than using the invoice as a substitute for approval.
  4. Send a specific reconciliation. State which line was checked, which records support the explanation, what correction was made if any, and what remains unresolved. Share only the relevant records through a private channel.
  5. Record the outcome without overstating it. Keep the response and corrected-document references. An explanation sent is not a dispute resolved; an approval receipt is not a payment receipt. If agreement remains unclear, seek qualified advice on the appropriate next step rather than inventing fees, deadlines, or collection rights.

A neutral clarification message

Thank you for flagging invoice [reference], line [reference]. I understand the mismatch concerns [specific issue]. I will compare it with change [reference/revision], the billing milestone, and prior invoice, credit, and payment records. Please identify any different version or record you are using. I will follow up by [realistic date] with the explanation, any correction, and any question still open.

Use only details you have verified and a follow-up date you can meet. Do not ask the customer to resend card or banking details. Keep payment information in the appropriate payment or accounting system.
