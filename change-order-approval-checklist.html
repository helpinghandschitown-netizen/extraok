Document the change before the extra work begins

A useful change record answers one question without a phone-call archaeology project: what changed, what will it cost, how will the schedule move, and what did the client decide?

Not legal advice. Required contract language, notices, signatures, cancellation rights, licensing rules, taxes, and record retention vary by jurisdiction and project. Check your contract and local requirements.

1. Anchor the original scope

  • Name the project, client, contractor, and original agreement or estimate.
  • Quote or summarize the specific original task that is changing.
  • Use a unique change number and issue date so later revisions cannot be confused.

2. Describe only the delta

  • State the newly discovered condition or client request in neutral, observable language.
  • List the additional work and explicitly list important exclusions.
  • Attach or separately retain supporting photos, measurements, and supplier notes when appropriate.
  • Do not hide allowances, assumptions, or work that still needs investigation.

3. Make the money auditable

  • Separate labor, materials, subcontractors, equipment, taxes, credits, and other allowed charges.
  • Show the exact net change and whether it is added to or subtracted from the contract amount.
  • Confirm who may authorize the cost and whether a deposit or payment milestone changes.

4. State the schedule impact

  • Give the estimated added or removed days and identify dependencies that could move the date.
  • Say whether the extra work blocks the next phase or can proceed in parallel.
  • Avoid promising dates that depend on permits, inspections, weather, or unavailable materials.

5. Capture an unambiguous decision

  • Present approve, decline, and request-revision as distinct choices.
  • Record the exact issued version, the decision, typed name, date, time, and any note.
  • Give both parties a copy. Never silently replace an already-issued version; issue a revision.

6. Preserve the record

  • Keep the original proposal, every revision, decision receipts, invoices, and related project correspondence together.
  • Export backups instead of trusting one phone, browser, inbox, or vendor account.
  • Share approval links privately because they can contain names, prices, addresses, and project details.

Before work resumes

  • The change is understandable without a verbal explanation.
  • The amount and schedule impact have been recalculated and reviewed.
  • The decision came from someone authorized to make it.
  • Any jurisdiction-specific notices or formalities are complete.
  • Both sides can retrieve the same final record.
