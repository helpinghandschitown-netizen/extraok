A change order was declined. What happens next?

A decline or revision request is a decision about the proposal you sent—not permission to perform a different version. Keep the original record, separate agreed work from disputed extras, and send a clearly identified replacement if the scope changes.

Operational guidance, not legal advice. Your contract and local rules govern notices, authorization, payment, suspension of work, and safety duties. This guide does not authorize stopping contracted work, withholding materials, or charging for unapproved extras.

1. Find out what the decision covers

Ask whether the concern is scope, cost, timing, or missing information. Quote the change number and version in your reply. Do not turn silence, a verbal discussion, or a request for more detail into an approval.

  • Declined: preserve the declined proposal and the client's response.
  • Revision requested: clarify the requested change before repricing.
  • Approved with a note: check whether the note introduces conditions that conflict with the proposal. Resolve ambiguity before treating it as agreement.

2. Separate the original job from the proposed extra

Write down which original tasks are unaffected and which depend on a decision. A declined extra does not by itself cancel the original agreement. If you encounter an unsafe condition, use the applicable safety and contract procedures rather than this checklist as authority to proceed or stop.

3. Build a replacement—not a silent edit

  • Retain the issued proposal and its decision receipt.
  • Give the replacement its own clear revision label and date, referencing the earlier proposal.
  • Restate the complete replacement scope, exclusions, price, and schedule impact. Do not make the client reconstruct them from several messages.
  • State whether the replacement supersedes the earlier proposal and whether any other changes remain separate.
  • Recheck quantities, credits, taxes where applicable, and dependencies before sending.

Example: reduce the scope without losing the record

Illustrative scenario, not a customer story: a painter proposes repairing damaged plaster and repainting an entire room. The client requests a smaller repair and localized paint touch-up instead.

The replacement should describe the smaller repair area, the touch-up boundary, and any expected color or sheen mismatch. It should state the replacement total and schedule impact. Keep the earlier room-repaint proposal as historical evidence; do not overwrite it to make it appear that the client accepted the smaller scope originally.

Copyable revision handoff record

Use this record when an estimator, office coordinator, and crew lead need the same answer about a changed proposal. Copy it into your project file or print this page. These are manual recordkeeping steps, not an automatic ExtraOK revision or notification feature.

  1. Identify both versions. Project: [reference]. Earlier proposal: [reference, version, issued date]. Replacement: [new reference, version, issued date]. Prepared by: [name]. Keep an unchanged copy of each issued proposal, not just its current link.
  2. Explain what changed. Requested by: [name and date]. Added scope: [details or none]. Removed scope: [details or none]. Exclusions: [details]. Replacement price and applicable tax treatment: [verified terms]. Schedule effect and dependencies: [verified details or explicitly unresolved]. State whether the price replaces the earlier total or is an additional amount; never leave the reader to guess.
  3. Separate proposal status from work authorization. Earlier proposal status: [declined, replacement proposed, or other documented outcome]. Replacement decision: [awaiting review, revision requested, declined, or approved]. Evidence: [exact proposal, response, date, responder, and authority checked]. Open conditions: [details or none]. A replacement being sent is not evidence that it was approved.
  4. Hand off the operational consequence. Original contracted work unaffected: [tasks]. Extra work awaiting a decision: [tasks]. Person responsible for clarification: [name]. Follow-up date: [date]. Crew and office notified: [who, when, and acknowledgment]. Apply your contract and safety procedures; this worksheet does not itself authorize work or a work stoppage.
  5. Retain and reconcile. Earlier proposal and response saved at: [private location]. Replacement and its decision evidence saved at: [private location]. Both parties given the same record on: [date]. Billing reference checked: [reference or pending]. Backup exported and checked on: [date]. Keep private links and project information out of public channels.

When the old link gets a new response

Do not silently attach an old proposal's response to the replacement. Identify exactly which version was reviewed, preserve the response, and ask the authorized parties to resolve any conflicting instructions. Record the clarification alongside both versions before relying on it for the affected extra work or billing. Do not claim that sending a replacement automatically disabled the earlier link.

Internal handoff message

Project [reference]: replacement [version] was sent on [date] for [brief change]. Its current decision is [status], supported by [record reference]. The earlier proposal [version] remains in the file with [documented outcome]. Unresolved items are [items]. [Owner] will confirm the affected work and billing instructions by [date]. Please use the identified version and flag any conflicting response rather than assuming approval.

Leave unresolved items visibly unresolved. A blank evidence field, an internal status label, or a copied message is not a client's decision.

A neutral message you can adapt

Thanks for reviewing change [reference/version]. I understand you want [requested adjustment]. I will prepare a replacement showing the revised scope, exclusions, price, and timing. The earlier proposal and your response will remain in the project record. Please review the replacement before authorizing the revised extra work. Separately, let's confirm how this affects the work already covered by our agreement.

Replace the brackets with verified details. Avoid promises about costs or dates until you have checked them.

4. Close the loop with both parties

  • Verify the responder is authorized for this project; possession of a link is not identity verification.
  • Keep the exact proposal reviewed, the decision, its note, and related correspondence together.
  • Give both sides the same final record and identify any superseded proposal.
  • If the extra is not going ahead, document that outcome and resolve any effect on the original scope under the contract.
